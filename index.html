Master’s capstone · June 2026

A Data Analysis Study of Temperature Based on California Cooperative Oceanic Fisheries Investigations (CALCOFI) Data

For my M.S. in Data Analysis & Visualization at the CUNY Graduate Center, I developed an analytical framework using long-term CALCOFI observations to study ocean-temperature patterns along the California coastline. I integrated data with Microsoft SQL Server and applied statistical modeling in R, comparing multiple regression approaches, including ridge regression.

Key result: The final multiple linear regression model achieved an adjusted R² of 0.86; depth, salinity, oxygen saturation, and nutrient concentrations together explained approximately 86% of temperature variation in the analyzed dataset. Advisor: Howard Everson.

Oceanography Environmental data science SQL Server R Regression Data visualization

Special Topics in Data Analysis and Visualization · Coursework Project

Population Concentration and Projection of New York City by Sex, Race, and Age

As part of my Special Topics in Data Analysis and Visualization coursework, I analyzed NYC population patterns by sex, race, and age. Using SQL, Excel, QGIS, and ArcGIS, I developed interactive maps and population projections, strengthening my skills in geospatial analysis and data visualization for research involving spatial analysis.

Key result: Interactive GIS maps revealed distinct demographic patterns across NYC neighborhoods, while population projections highlighted potential changes by race, age, and sex over time.

Decision Tree for Predicting the Party of Legislators

Individual quantitative research poster using 2018–2019 U.S. House roll-call voting datasets to predict legislators’ party affiliation with a decision-tree model. Python was used to collect data, SAS to clean it, and R for calculations and graphical presentation.

Quantitative research poster · December 2020

Principal Component Analysis for Predicting the Party of the Legislators

Extended the earlier legislator-voting project by applying Principal Component Analysis (PCA), an unsupervised learning method, to 2018–2019 U.S. House roll-call vote data. The project explored a compressed representation for distinguishing party affiliation and compared PCA with decision-tree classification, using R and Excel for data analysis.

Recognition: Second place, STEM individual projects, at the 33rd Semi-Annual Dr. Janet Liou-Mark Honors and Undergraduate Research Scholars Poster Presentation, December 3, 2020. Mentor: Professor Nan Li (Mathematics).
